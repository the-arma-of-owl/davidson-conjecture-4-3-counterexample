# A counterexample to Davidson's Conjecture 4.3: a non-3-colourable monomial Cayley graph with an algebraic noninteger direction parameter

*Efe Çam — with AI assistance (see "How this was produced" at the end). Version 1.3, 26 November 2026. Version 1.1 added the Lean formalization. Version 1.2 added an explicit finite witness (Remark 1) and a second check by GPT-6.2 Astra. Version 1.3 adds a lower bound for finite witnesses (Remark 3). The proof of the Theorem is unchanged (see `CHANGELOG.md`).*

## Abstract

For a unit complex number $\alpha$, let $\Gamma_\alpha=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb Z[\alpha],\{\pm\alpha^n:n\ge0\})$. In this graph two points of $\mathbb Z[\alpha]\subset\mathbb C$ are adjacent when they differ by $\pm\alpha^n$, so all edges have length one. J. Davidson [D] proved $\chi(\Gamma_\alpha)\le 4$ for every algebraic noninteger $\alpha$, and $\chi(\Gamma_\alpha)\le3$ when the leading coefficient of the primitive minimal polynomial is at least $3$. He conjectured that three colours always suffice for algebraic nonintegers ([D], Conjecture 4.3). He showed that any counterexample has leading coefficient two and degree at least ten.

We show that the conjecture is false.

> **Theorem.** Let $\alpha$ be any root of
> $$f(x)=2x^{12}+2x^{11}+x^{10}-x^{9}-2x^{8}-x^{7}-x^{6}-x^{5}-2x^{4}-x^{3}+x^{2}+2x+2 .$$
> Then $\alpha$ is an algebraic noninteger direction parameter, and $\chi(\Gamma_\alpha)=4$.

The lower bound combines two ingredients:

- **A three-colour criterion for abelian Cayley graphs.** It goes through integer height functions and a Hahn–Banach argument. Its forward direction is formalised in Lean in [K], and again, independently, in our Lean development. With it, three-colourability of the subgraph generated by $1,\alpha,\dots,\alpha^{79}$ reduces to a finite disjunction of linear feasibility problems.
- **An exact certificate of infeasibility.** It is a search tree of $75{,}530$ nodes, each carrying rational Farkas multipliers, and it was checked by independent exact checkers.
- **A formal proof.** The refutation is formalized in Lean 4 as the theorem `conjecture43_false`. Its statement of the conjecture uses the definitions of §1. Every step is checked by the Lean kernel except one: acceptance of the certificate, which uses `native_decide` (§4, "Formal verification").

The threshold is sharp: the subgraph generated by $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{78}$ is three-colourable.

## 1. Setting

Following [D], a *direction parameter* is a unit complex number $\alpha$, and $\Gamma_\alpha=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb Z[\alpha],\{\pm\alpha^n : n\ge 0\})$. For algebraic $\alpha$, let $f=\sum_{i=0}^d a_ix^i$ be its primitive integer minimal polynomial with $a_d>0$. Then $\alpha$ is *noninteger* if $a_d\ge2$, i.e. if $\alpha$ is not an algebraic integer.

**[D, Conjecture 4.3].** If $\alpha$ is an algebraic noninteger direction parameter, then $\chi(\Gamma_\alpha)\le 3$.

[D, Prop. 4.5] shows that a counterexample must satisfy all of the following:

- $\chi(\Gamma_\alpha)=4$;
- $a_d=a_0=2$;
- the trace polynomial is $F(y)=y(y^2-4)Q(y)\pm1$, with $Q$ having leading coefficient $2$;
- $\Phi_m\nmid f\pmod 3$ for all $m$;
- $D_8\in\{1,7\}$.

By [D, Cor. 4.6] the degree is at least ten. [D, §5] notes that "only algebraic nonintegers with leading coefficient two and degree at least ten remain."

## 2. The polynomial

Write $a=(a_0,\dots,a_{12})=(2,2,1,-1,-2,-1,-1,-1,-2,-1,1,2,2)$.

- $f$ is palindromic, primitive, and irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
- $a_{12}=a_0=2$ and $f(1)=1$.
- Its trace polynomial, with $x^{6}F(x+x^{-1})=f(x)$, is
  $$F(y)=2y^6+2y^5-11y^4-11y^3+12y^2+12y+1=y(y^2-4)(y+1)(2y^2-3)+1 .$$
- $F$ has six distinct real roots in $(-2,2)$, so all twelve roots of $f$ lie on the unit circle, at arguments $\pm5.29^\circ,\pm51.17^\circ,\pm92.65^\circ,\pm113.16^\circ,\pm134.18^\circ,\pm170.50^\circ$.
- Since $f$ is not monic, no root is an algebraic integer, and in particular no root is a root of unity.

So every root $\alpha$ is an algebraic noninteger direction parameter.

$f$ satisfies every restriction of [D, Prop. 4.5]:

- $F(-2)=F(0)=F(2)=1$;
- $Q=(y+1)(2y^2-3)$ has leading coefficient $2$;
- $F\equiv A+By \pmod{y^2-2}$ with $D_8=|A^2-2B^2|=1$;
- no cyclotomic polynomial divides $f$ modulo $3$.

## 3. The three-colour criterion

For $K\ge 12$, let $M_K=\mathbb Z\langle 1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^K\rangle\subset\mathbb Z[\alpha]$, and let $G_K=\mathrm{Cay}(M_K,\{\pm\alpha^n:0\le n\le K\})$. Then $G_K$ is a subgraph of $\Gamma_\alpha$.

**Lemma 1 (relations).** Let $\pi:\mathbb Z^{K+1}\to M_K$ be $e_n\mapsto\alpha^n$. Then $\ker\pi$ is generated by the coefficient vectors $\ell_j$ of $x^jf(x)$, for $0\le j\le K-12$.

*Proof.* An integer polynomial of degree at most $K$ vanishing at $\alpha$ is a $\mathbb Q[x]$-multiple of $f$. Since $f$ is primitive, Gauss's lemma makes it a $\mathbb Z[x]$-multiple. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (necessity).** If $G_K$ is properly $3$-colourable, then there are reals $\sigma_0,\dots,\sigma_K\in[-1,1]$ such that
$$\tau_j:=\sum_{i=0}^{12}a_i\,\sigma_{i+j}\ \in\ 3(2\mathbb Z+1)\qquad(0\le j\le K-12).$$

*Proof.* **Lift and height function.** Let $c:M_K\to\mathbb Z/3$ be a proper colouring, and let $\hat c=c\circ\pi$ on the grid graph $\mathbb Z^{K+1}$ (steps $\pm e_n$); $\hat c$ is proper. For an edge $u\to u\pm e_n$, put $\delta(u,u\pm e_n)=\pm1$ according to whether $\hat c$ increases by $+1$ or $-1$ modulo $3$.

- **No winding on unit squares.** Around a unit square the four values of $\delta$ sum to a multiple of $3$ that lies in $\{-4,-2,0,2,4\}$, hence to $0$.
- **A height function.** The cycle space of the grid is generated by its unit squares, so $\delta$ integrates to $h:\mathbb Z^{K+1}\to\mathbb Z$ with $h\equiv\hat c\pmod 3$ and $|h(u)-h(v)|=1$ on edges.
- **Constant shifts.** For $\ell\in\ker\pi$ we have $\hat c(\cdot+\ell)=\hat c$. Hence $\delta$ is $\ell$-invariant, and $h(v+\ell)-h(v)=:\tau(\ell)$ does not depend on $v$. The map $\tau:\ker\pi\to\mathbb Z$ is additive.

**Arithmetic of $\tau$.**

- $\tau(\ell)\equiv 0\pmod3$, because $h\equiv\hat c\pmod 3$.
- $\tau(\ell)\equiv\sum_n\ell_n\pmod 2$, because each step changes the parity of $h$.
- $|\tau(\ell)|\le\|\ell\|_1$, because $h$ is $1$-Lipschitz for the graph distance, which is the $\ell_1$ distance.
- Since $\sum_n(\ell_j)_n=f(1)=1$, each $\tau(\ell_j)$ is an odd multiple of $3$.

**Extension.** Extend $\tau$ $\mathbb Q$-linearly to $\ker\pi\otimes\mathbb Q$. For $x$ in this rational span, choose $m\ge1$ with $mx\in\ker\pi$; then $|\tau(x)|=|\tau(mx)|/m\le\|mx\|_1/m=\|x\|_1$. The extension is therefore $1$-Lipschitz, and by density it extends continuously to a linear functional on $\ker\pi\otimes\mathbb R$ with $|\tau(x)|\le\|x\|_1$. By Hahn–Banach it extends further to $\sigma\in(\mathbb R^{K+1})^*$ with $\|\sigma\|_\infty\le1$. Then $\tau_j=\tau(\ell_j)=\sum_i a_i\sigma_{i+j}$. $\square$

**Lemma 2$'$ (converse).** If $\sigma\in[-1,1]^{K+1}$ satisfies $\sum_i a_i\sigma_{i+j}\in3(2\mathbb Z+1)$ for all $j$, then $G_K$ is $3$-colourable.

*Proof.* Let $p(x)=\sum_n x_n \bmod 2$, and let $h(x)$ be the element of $2\mathbb Z+p(x)$ nearest to $\sigma\cdot x$, with ties broken upwards. By additivity, $\sigma\cdot\ell\in\mathbb Z$, $\sigma\cdot\ell\equiv\sum\ell_n\pmod2$ and $\sigma\cdot\ell\equiv0\pmod3$ for every $\ell\in\ker\pi$, so $h(x+\ell)=h(x)+\sigma\cdot\ell$. Moreover $h(x\pm e_n)-h(x)$ is odd and of absolute value $<3$, hence $\pm1$. Thus $h \bmod 3$ is a proper, $\ker\pi$-invariant colouring of the grid, i.e. a $3$-colouring of $G_K$. $\square$

Equivalently, $\theta(\alpha^n)=(3+\sigma_n)/6$ defines a character of $M_K$ with every generator in $[1/3,2/3]$. This is the criterion of [K, row 79] ("three colours are linear"). Its forward direction is proved there in the Lean kernel (`ThreeColourCharacter.bandSet_three_nonempty_of_proper`), and the converse also holds [K]. The lower bound uses only Lemma 2. Lemma 2$'$ is used only for the sharpness statement and Remark 1. The height-function ingredient is classical: Krebs–Sankar's winding homomorphism and Chandgotia's height functions; see the prior-art notes in [K].

## 4. The certificate

**Proposition 3.** For $K=79$ there is no $\sigma\in[-1,1]^{80}$ with $\tau_j=\sum_{i=0}^{12}a_i\sigma_{i+j}\in3(2\mathbb Z+1)$ for all $0\le j\le 67$.

*Certificate.* **Reformulation.** Each $|\tau_j|\le\sum|a_i|=19$, so $\tau_j\in T=\{\pm3,\pm9,\pm15\}$. Since $\sigma\mapsto-\sigma$ negates every $\tau_j$, we may assume $\tau_0>0$. Put $x=(\sigma_0,\dots,\sigma_{11})\in[-1,1]^{12}$. Given a word $w=(\tau_0,\dots,\tau_{j-1})$, the equations determine
$$\sigma_{k+12}=\frac{\tau_k-c_k(x)}{2},\qquad c_k(x)=\sum_{i<12}a_i\sigma_{i+k},$$
as affine functions of $x$. The constraint $\sigma_{k+12}\in[-1,1]$ becomes $|c_k(x)-\tau_k|\le 2$.

**The tree.** For a word $w$ let $P_w=\{x\in[-1,1]^{12}:|c_k(x)-\tau_k|\le 2\ \text{for } k<|w|\}$. The certificate is a tree of words in which every node $w$ carries rational multipliers $y$ of one of two kinds:

- *(E)* a Farkas certificate that the polytope $P_w$ is empty; or
- *(I)* certificates of an interval $[lb,ub]$ containing $c_{|w|}(P_w)$. Every admissible $\tau$ with $[\tau-2,\tau+2]\cap[lb,ub]\neq\emptyset$ must then appear as a child, where the admissible values are $\{3,9,15\}$ at the root and $T$ below it.

Nodes at depth $68$ must be of type (E). A valid tree therefore shows that no word of length $68$ is feasible.

**Size.** The tree has $75{,}530$ nodes, maximal depth $67$, and takes $13.9$ MB compressed. The file is `proof_fB_L68.jsonl.gz` in `certificate/`, and the sha256 of the uncompressed file is `19602d8d…b27e`. The multipliers were proposed by HiGHS and are stored as exact rationals.

**Checks.**

- `check_proof.py` (pure `fractions`, independent of the generator) accepts the tree in 148 s, and it rejects truncated or corrupted files.
- An independent referee re-implemented the checker; see `VERIFICATION.md`. Its exact checker passes in 19.5 s. It also rebuilt the search from scratch with a different LP solver (GLPK), a different formulation and a different certificate type. That search produced exactly the same set of $75{,}530$ nodes, and a separate exact verifier accepts it. The same search without the symmetry reduction gives $151{,}059=2\cdot75{,}530-1$ nodes and also passes.
- GPT-6.2 Astra re-checked all $75{,}530$ nodes with its own integer-arithmetic checker; see `verification/astra/`. It repeated the check at a later commit, when it also audited the Lean proof; see `verification/astra_3f96a35/`.
- MILP solvers agree: HiGHS reports $K=79,80$ infeasible and $K\le78$ feasible, and SCIP 10.1 (floating-point mode) reports $K=79$ infeasible and $K=78$ feasible.

**Sharpness.** `certificate/fB_K78_feasible_cert.json` gives an exact rational $\sigma$ and a word $\tau$ for $K=78$; the referee confirmed it and produced two further exact solutions with SCIP. By Lemma 2$'$, $G_{78}$ is $3$-colourable.

**Proof of the Theorem.** By Proposition 3 and Lemma 2, $G_{79}$ is not $3$-colourable, so $\chi(\Gamma_\alpha)\ge\chi(G_{79})\ge4$. By [D, Thm 4.1], $\chi(\Gamma_\alpha)\le 4$. $\square$

**Formal verification.** The directory `lean/` contains a Lean 4 (Mathlib) formalization. With $\Gamma_\alpha$ defined as $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb Z[\alpha],\{\pm\alpha^n\})$ on the subring $\mathbb Z[\alpha]\subset\mathbb C$, it proves:

```lean
def Conjecture43 : Prop :=
  ∀ α : ℂ, ‖α‖ = 1 → IsAlgebraic ℚ α → ¬ IsIntegral ℤ α → (Gamma α).chromaticNumber ≤ 3
theorem conjecture43_false : ¬ Conjecture43
theorem root_counterexample (α : ℂ) (hα : aeval α f = 0) :
    ‖α‖ = 1 ∧ IsAlgebraic ℚ α ∧ ¬ IsIntegral ℤ α ∧ 4 ≤ (Gamma α).chromaticNumber
```

The Lean kernel checks the following:

- Lemma 2, for every $K$, via a height function on $\mathbb Z^{K+1}$ and the Hahn–Banach theorem.
- The soundness of a certificate checker. Soundness holds for every certificate tree, so neither the parser nor the data has to be trusted.
- That no root of $f$ is an algebraic integer. If $\alpha$ were one, then so would be $\bar\alpha=\alpha^{-1}$, and hence $\alpha+\alpha^{-1}$, which is a root of $F$. If a root of $F$ were integral, its monic minimal polynomial $g$ would divide $F$ in $\mathbb Z[x]$. Since $F$ is irreducible modulo $3$, this forces $F=2g$, which is impossible because $F(0)=1$ is odd. This argument does not need the irreducibility of $f$.
- The facts of §2 used above.

One step uses `native_decide`: the claim that the checker accepts the $75{,}530$-node certificate. It is established by compiled evaluation, so the Lean compiler is trusted for that step. Three separately written exact checkers in this repository also accept the same certificate: `code/check_proof.py`, the referee's `verification/referee_code/ref_check.py`, and GPT-6.2 Astra's `verification/astra/davidson.py`.

Not formalized, and not needed for the refutation:

- the upper bound $\chi(\Gamma_\alpha)\le4$ [D, Thm 4.1];
- Lemma 2$'$ and sharpness;
- the irreducibility of $f$ over $\mathbb Q$.

The conjecture's hypothesis "noninteger" is formalized as "not an algebraic integer"; by Gauss's lemma this is equivalent to $a_d\ge2$ (§1).

## 5. Remarks

1. **Finite witnesses.** Some finite subgraph of $G_{79}$ is $4$-chromatic. This follows from de Bruijn–Erdős [dBE], and explicitly from the construction below. Any such subgraph must use a step $\alpha^n$ with $n\ge 79$: the steps $\alpha^n$ with $n\le78$ preserve the cosets of $M_{78}$ in $\mathbb Z[\alpha]$, and each coset induces a copy of the $3$-colourable graph $G_{78}$.

   *An explicit witness* (GPT-6.2 Astra; `verification/astra_3f96a35/`). Let $B=\{-10,\dots,10\}^{80}$, and let $S$ be the graph on $\pi(B)$ whose edges are the images of the unit edges of $B$. Then $S$ is not $3$-colourable.

   *Proof.* Let $A$ be the $68\times80$ matrix with rows $\ell_0,\dots,\ell_{67}$. Every row and every column of $A$ has absolute sum at most $19$.

   - **Heights.** Pull a $3$-colouring of $S$ back to $B$. As in Lemma 2 it gives a height function $h$ on $B$, because closed walks in a box are generated by its unit squares.
   - **Jumps.** For each $j$, the difference $h(x+\ell_j)-h(x)$ is constant on the box $B\cap(B-\ell_j)$. Call it $t_j$. As in Lemma 2, $t_j\in3(2\mathbb Z+1)$.
   - **A closed walk.** Fix $m\in\mathbb Z^{68}$ and put $z=A^{\mathsf T}m$. Take $|m_j|$ jumps $\operatorname{sgn}(m_j)\,\ell_j$ for each $j$ and $|z_i|$ unit steps $-\operatorname{sgn}(z_i)\,e_i$ for each $i$. These moves sum to zero.
   - **It stays in $B$.** Schedule the $N$ copies of each kind of move at the times $(k-\tfrac12)/N$, $k=1,\dots,N$, and perform all moves in time order, starting at $0$. At every moment, each kind has been used within $\tfrac12$ of its proportional share, and the proportional shares sum to zero. So coordinate $i$ stays within $\tfrac12\bigl(\sum_j|(\ell_j)_i|+1\bigr)\le10$ of $0$.
   - **The inequality.** Along the walk, $h$ changes by $\pm t_j$ at each jump and by $\pm1$ at each unit step, and it returns to its starting value. Hence $|m\cdot t|\le\|A^{\mathsf T}m\|_1$. By scaling and continuity this holds for all real $m$.
   - **Contradiction.** Since $\|A^{\mathsf T}m\|_1=\max_{\sigma\in[-1,1]^{80}}m\cdot A\sigma$, the separation theorem gives $t=A\sigma$ for some $\sigma\in[-1,1]^{80}$. This contradicts Proposition 3. $\square$

   **Size.** Every vertex of $S$ can be written as $\sum_{k=12}^{79}r_k\alpha^k+\sum_{i<12}u_i\alpha^i$ with $r_k\in\{0,1\}$, because $a_{12}=2$. Each $u_i$ takes boundedly many values. So $S$ has fewer than $7.95\times10^{52}$ vertices (`witness_bound.py`), which is far too many to list. The construction is not formalized in Lean. We know of no small explicit witness. The sumset vertex sets we tried (up to about $2.5$ million vertices) induced $3$-colourable graphs.
2. **Each equation is needed.** Deleting any one of the $68$ equations of Proposition 3 leaves a feasible system. For each $j$, GPT-6.2 Astra gave an exact rational $\sigma\in[-1,1]^{80}$ that satisfies the other $67$ equations (`verification/astra_3f96a35/omission_witnesses.json`); we re-checked all $68$. So no proper subset of the equations suffices.
3. **A lower bound for finite witnesses.** Every finite subgraph $S$ of $G_{79}$ that is not $3$-colourable contains a cycle of length at least $377$, so it has at least $377$ vertices.

   *Proof.* Lift a closed walk $\gamma$ in $S$ to a walk in the grid $\mathbb Z^{80}$. By Lemma 1 the lift ends at its start plus $A^{\mathsf T}m(\gamma)$ for a unique $m(\gamma)\in\mathbb Z^{68}$, and $|\gamma|\ge\|A^{\mathsf T}m(\gamma)\|_1$. Let $t\in3(2\mathbb Z+1)^{68}$. Since $f(1)=1$, both $|\gamma|$ and $t\cdot m(\gamma)$ have the parity of $\sum_jm_j(\gamma)$.

   - **Colouring from $t$.** Suppose $|t\cdot m(\gamma)|\le|\gamma|$ for every closed walk $\gamma$ in $S$. Fix a lift $\tilde v\in\mathbb Z^{80}$ of each vertex $v$. An edge $vw$ lifts to a grid edge from $\tilde v$ to $\tilde w+\ell_{vw}$ with $\ell_{vw}\in\ker\pi$. We look for integers $H(v)$ of the parity of $\sum_n\tilde v_n$ with $|H(w)-H(v)+t\cdot m(\ell_{vw})|\le1$ on every edge. Writing $H=2G+\text{parity}$, this is a system of difference constraints on $G$ with integer bounds. Around a closed walk the bounds add up to $(|\gamma|\mp t\cdot m(\gamma))/2\ge0$, so the system has a solution. Each $H(w)-H(v)+t\cdot m(\ell_{vw})$ is odd, hence $\pm1$, and $t\cdot m\equiv0\pmod 3$. So $H\bmod 3$ is a proper $3$-colouring of $S$.
   - **A long cycle.** Hence, if $S$ is not $3$-colourable, then for every $t$ it contains a closed walk with $|t\cdot m(\gamma)|>|\gamma|$. Splitting at repeated vertices gives such a cycle, and by parity $|t\cdot m(\gamma)|\ge|\gamma|+2$.
   - **The certificate.** `data/floor_certificates.json` gives a $t$ and an exact $\sigma$ with $A\sigma=t$ and $\rho=\|\sigma\|_\infty=1.00531\ldots$. Then $|\gamma|+2\le|t\cdot m|=|\sigma\cdot A^{\mathsf T}m|\le\rho\,\|A^{\mathsf T}m\|_1\le\rho|\gamma|$, so $|\gamma|\ge2/(\rho-1)>376$. $\square$

   The other certificates in the same file give at least $345$ vertices for $G_{83}$, $144$ for $G_{90}$ and $68$ for $G_{120}$. `code/check_floor.py` checks all four exactly, and `code/floor_search.py` found them with HiGHS. Together with Remark 1, the smallest non-$3$-colourable finite subgraph of $G_{79}$ has between $377$ and $7.95\times10^{52}$ vertices. This remark is not formalized in Lean.
4. **Calibration.** The same computation for the monic sextic $x^6-x^4-x^3-x^2+1$ of [D] gives threshold $K=12$. This matches the exponent range of the 24-vertex witness of [D]. There the argument of Remark 3 gives at least $10$ vertices, consistent with that witness.
5. **Other candidates.** Among the candidates with $F=y(y^2-4)Q\pm1$ that pass [D]'s filters, every one we examined has a periodic three-colouring, with these exceptions:
   - $f$ and its mirror $f(-x)$ (all candidates of degree $\le 12$ with coefficients bounded by $16$);
   - a degree-14 candidate, still undecided;
   - a few sparse higher-degree ones, still undecided.

   See `data/`.
